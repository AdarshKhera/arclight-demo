Who it is for, and the one rule

Emma, on the night shift, with five tools open.

Emma is a critical facilities engineer. On a normal night she has alarms in one tool, racks in another, work orders in a third, contracts on a shared drive and visitors on a clipboard.

Arclight puts the campus in the middle and hangs every job off it. My one rule was become the user: every round, I opened it as Emma at midnight and did a real job. Where I hesitated, the design was wrong.

The mark

Three rounds to find a logo.

chosenThe rack arc: three server slabs under one arc that bends down into a leg. The top slab is the one that lights up, so the mark works as a status light too. It is on the sidebar, the sign-in card, the favicon and the entrance sign in the 3D campus.

Additions, subtractions, honesty

What changed, and what is sample.

addedSite access with two approvers and PIN passes, revenue and SLA credits on the dashboard, a pitch-first tour.
cutThe seven-step tour of buttons, the flat menu, banners over page buttons. Two logo rounds that did not make it.
nextReal door events from the access control system, readings from BMS and Redfish, real billing, and more than one site.
sampleThe campus, its faults and forecasts are simulated. Agreements, customers and revenue are written from common terms, not real ones.

How it is built

I direct, Claude Code writes.

Arclight is one HTML file with Three.js for the campus and no backend. Everything runs and saves in the browser, and the PINs are hashed before they are saved. I wrote the product rules, played the user every round, and judged every screen.

Try the live prototype. It runs in your browser with sample data, on desktop or phone.

The hard part was never the 3D. It was deciding what a tired engineer needs first at 2 am.
